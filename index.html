Signal to Launch

A decision story about translating scattered evidence into a product direction that commercial and operational teams can actually carry. This reflects professional practice without exposing employer-confidential information or invented outcomes.

01 / Ambiguity

The problem crosses teams.

A customer need rarely arrives neatly packaged. It appears through feedback, market movement, competitor activity, internal performance, supplier realities, and service constraints.

02 / Signals

Make the evidence legible.

Connect market, customer, commercial, analytics, supply-chain, and operational inputs. Separate facts from assumptions and expose the dependencies behind the opportunity.

03 / Decision

Distil a credible direction.

Translate the system into positioning, a value proposition, requirements, priorities, trade-offs, and a next step that can be explained and challenged.

04 / Alignment

Prepare more than the launch.

Coordinate Sales, marketing, analytics, supply chain, suppliers, and operational partners around readiness, adoption, handovers, and the reality after release.

05 / Learning

Let performance change the plan.

Use KPIs, service levels, availability, customer feedback, and post-launch signals to decide what to adjust, protect, or stop.

Integrity note. Scope and decision value are described here without invented metrics, client names, portfolio values, team sizes, or confidential details.

Independent concept · B2B mobility data

Circular Mobility Intelligence

A hypothesis for helping fleet and mobility partners turn vehicle-use signals into clearer decisions about maintenance, utilisation, downtime, asset life, and circular value.

Problem

Signals may exist across systems, but the decision context linking them to maintenance, utilisation, and asset life can remain fragmented.

Product hypothesis

A focused, interpretable workspace could help fleet partners identify questions worth acting on without automating every operational judgement.

First scope

One decision journey around asset health and utilisation, with candidate inputs, uncertainty, and human review made explicit.

Next validation

Discovery with representative partners, definition of a minimum useful signal set, prototype testing, and technical feasibility review.

Measures to test

Decision usefulness, task completion, confidence in interpretation, and whether information supports timely operational conversations.

Responsibility

Data minimisation, consent, privacy, interoperability, energy use, incomplete signals, and the risk of shifting cost elsewhere in the system.

Independent concept · Active cities · Barcelona

Outside / In

A personal product experiment connecting movement, nature, public mobility, accessible routes, wellbeing, and responsible urban discovery.

Problem

Discovery tools can privilege novelty and speed over access, route context, public mobility, recovery needs, and pressure on shared spaces.

Product hypothesis

A calmer planning layer could help people choose experiences that fit their energy, time, access needs, and impact.

First scope

Clear route context, accessibility cues, public-mobility options, and respectful-use guidance rather than a broad social network.

Next validation

Route research, conversations with local users and access advocates, service blueprinting, and low-fidelity journey testing.

Measures to test

Planning confidence, comprehension of options, usefulness of accessibility information, and lower-impact choices when feasible.

Responsibility

Route safety, inclusion, seasonal change, overcrowding, local stewardship, data restraint, and the limits of recommendations.

Circular Mobility Intelligence is an independent concept. It is not affiliated with or commissioned by Volkswagen Group.

The human engine / Endurance becomes execution

Pace is a product decision.

Mountain trails and endurance sport shaped how I work: read the terrain, protect the pace, adapt before failure, and spend energy where it changes the outcome.

Progress is not the loudest sprint. It is the sequence of decisions that still makes sense at kilometre thirty-five.
01 / Prioritise

Protect the signal.

Focus under pressure means choosing the few moves that preserve customer value, commercial purpose, and delivery energy. Activity is not the same as progress.

02 / Align

Move as one system.

Endurance is collective in product work. Clear dependencies, informed handovers, and honest pacing let several functions carry one direction.

03 / Learn

Adapt before failure.

Feedback, service levels, recovery, and iteration are not signs that the plan was weak. They are how a product remains useful under changing conditions.

Biomimetic truth, made practical.

Nature is a design framework, not a promise of perfect outcomes. It asks whether energy is being wasted, whether outputs can become inputs, whether the system can adapt, and whether value externalises unnecessary cost. In product terms: circular lifecycles, resource awareness, durable commercial value, honest trade-offs, and responsibility for data and consequences.
